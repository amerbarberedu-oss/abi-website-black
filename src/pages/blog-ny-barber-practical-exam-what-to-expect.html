The NY Barber Practical Exam: What to Expect and How to Prepare

From the American Barber Institute blog.

The New York barber practical exam is the hands-on test you must pass to get your barber license from the NY Department of State (DOS). You schedule it online after your license application is approved, pay a $15 non-refundable fee, and show up on time with valid government photo ID. To prepare, study the official supply list, tasks evaluated and draping procedures that DOS publishes, then practice those tasks until they're routine. Here's what to expect, step by step.

When can you take the NY barber practical exam?

Not until your application is approved. The order looks like this: you finish your training (a 500-hour program at a school like ABI, or a two-year registered apprenticeship), complete the state-approved contagious diseases course, get your physical signed, and submit your license application with the $40 fee. Once DOS approves the application, it emails you a link to schedule the practical exam online.

One timing rule to keep in mind: the application has to be submitted within 30 days of the date your physician, physician's assistant or nurse practitioner signs the health certification. And you must pass the practical exam within two years. If any of this is new to you, start with our full guide, How to Get a Barber License in New York.

Scheduling, rescheduling and fees

The exam fee is $15, paid when you schedule, and it's non-refundable. The rules around changing your date are strict, so read them twice:

  • Rescheduling is free if you do it at least 6 days before your exam.
  • Cancelling or not showing up forfeits your $15 fee.
  • Arriving late also forfeits the fee, and late arrivals are not admitted. There's no squeezing in after the start time.

Plan your route the day before, account for train delays, and aim to arrive early. Losing your slot and your fee because the train stalled is a frustrating, avoidable setback.

What ID do you need?

Bring a current, unexpired government-issued photo ID that includes your signature. The name on your ID must match the name on your exam appointment. If your name has changed, or your ID is close to expiring, sort it out well before exam day. A mismatch is one of the easiest ways to get turned away, and it's completely avoidable.

If you need testing accommodations, you can request them from DOS using form DOS-1591. Do this early so it's in place before you schedule.

What's on the practical exam?

DOS publishes three documents on dos.ny.gov that tell you exactly what the exam covers, and they're the best preparation material you'll find anywhere:

  • The practical exam supply list: everything you're expected to bring.
  • The list of tasks evaluated: what the examiners will watch you do and score.
  • The draping procedures: how the state expects you to drape your client.

Download all three as soon as you start preparing, and check them again shortly before your exam in case anything has been updated. We're not going to list the tasks here, because the official list is the one that counts and it can change. Treat those documents as your checklist.

How to prepare for the barber practical exam

1. Build your kit from the supply list

Go line by line through the supply list and pack your kit well ahead of time. Use the same tools on exam day that you practiced with. Showing up with an unfamiliar tool, or missing an item, adds stress you don't need.

2. Practice every evaluated task, in order

Take the list of tasks evaluated and run through it start to finish, the same way you'll do it on exam day. Then do it again. The goal is that nothing on the list feels new. If one task makes you nervous, that's the one to practice most.

3. Make sanitation automatic

Infection control and sanitation are at the heart of New York barbering, which is why the state requires a contagious diseases course before you're licensed. Clean, disinfect and handle tools correctly every single time you practice, so you do it without thinking when someone is watching.

4. Rehearse draping

Draping is easy to overlook because it isn't a haircut. Follow the official draping procedures exactly when you practice, so you're not improvising in front of an examiner.

5. Do a full mock run

Set up a practice session that feels like the real thing: your packed kit, the official task list, a timer and someone watching. It's the closest you can get to the pressure of exam day.

Exam-day checklist

  • Valid, unexpired government photo ID with signature, name matching your appointment.
  • Your full kit, checked against the official supply list.
  • A final review of the tasks evaluated and draping procedures.
  • A travel plan that gets you there early. Late means no entry and a lost fee.
