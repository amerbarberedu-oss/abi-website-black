How to Pay for Barber School in NYC: ACCES-VR, VA Benefits and Payment Plans

From the American Barber Institute blog.

Most people pay for barber school in New York City with a weekly payment plan, often while they keep working. Some qualify for help that covers part or all of the cost: ACCES-VR, New York State's vocational rehabilitation program, can pay for training for eligible New Yorkers with a documented disability, and veterans and their families can use GI Bill and other VA education benefits at approved schools. Here is how each option works, what tuition actually looks like, and how to plan for the costs people tend to forget.

How much does barber school cost in NYC?

Prices vary a lot from school to school, so always ask for the full tuition, what it includes and what it doesn't. At American Barber Institute, the 500-Hour Master Barber Program is the same 500 hours of training on every schedule, but the price depends on which schedule you choose:

  • Afternoon (Mon–Fri, 2 PM–8 PM): $3,600, about 17 weeks. This is the most popular schedule.
  • Weekend (Sat–Sun, 9 AM–7 PM): $4,600, about 27 weeks (6–7 months).
  • Morning (Mon–Fri, 8 AM–2 PM): $5,600, about 17 weeks.

The $100 non-refundable registration fee is included in those numbers. Both campuses, Manhattan and the Bronx, offer all three schedules. You can compare them side by side on the schedules page.

Weekly payment plans: paying as you go

For most students, the weekly payment plan is what makes school possible. Instead of paying the full tuition up front, you start from $200 down and then pay weekly while you attend, with plans from $160 a week. Payment plans are available to everyone. You don't need to qualify for a program to use one.

A weekly plan works well because it lines up with how most people get paid. If you know your payment amount before you start, you can build it into your budget the same way you would rent or a phone bill. Before you enroll, ask admissions to walk you through the exact weekly amount for your schedule so there are no surprises.

Can ACCES-VR pay for barber school?

It can, if you qualify. ACCES-VR (Adult Career and Continuing Education Services – Vocational Rehabilitation) is part of the New York State Education Department. It helps New Yorkers with a documented disability prepare for and get a job. For eligible participants, ACCES-VR can cover tuition, fees, books and tools for approved training, which can mean finishing barber school with little or no out-of-pocket cost.

The process runs through ACCES-VR, not through the school. In general you apply with ACCES-VR, go through their eligibility process, and work with a counselor on an employment plan. If barbering is part of that plan, the training can be approved and arranged with the school. Timelines depend on ACCES-VR, so if you think you may qualify, start early. ABI works with ACCES-VR students regularly; you can read more on our ACCES-VR program page.

Using the GI Bill and VA benefits for barber school

Veterans, service members and eligible family members can use VA education benefits for barber training at approved schools. ABI's Manhattan campus is approved for:

  • Post-9/11 GI Bill (Chapter 33)
  • Veteran Readiness and Employment, VR&E (Chapter 31)
  • Montgomery GI Bill
  • Survivors' and Dependents' Educational Assistance, DEA (Chapter 35)

Please note: VA benefits are accepted at the Manhattan campus only, not in the Bronx. If you plan to use your benefits, you'll enroll at 48 West 39th Street.

To get started you'll need your Certificate of Eligibility from the VA and your DD-214. If you don't have your Certificate of Eligibility yet, apply for it through the VA first, since the school can't process your benefits without it. More details are on the veterans page.

Budget for tools, books and supplies

Tuition isn't the only cost. Tools, books and supplies are extra. You'll get the tool list at registration, and you can buy your kit from ABI or anywhere else. Clippers, trimmers, shears, a straight razor, combs, brushes and capes are tools you'll keep using after you graduate, so treat them as an investment in your career, not just a school expense.

There are also a few licensing costs to plan for after graduation. New York requires the 3-hour contagious diseases course for every new barber application (ABI offers it as a home-study course by mail for $100). The State charges a $15 fee when you schedule the practical exam and a $40 initial license application fee. Small amounts, but it helps to set them aside ahead of time. Our guide on how to get a barber license in New York covers each step.

Working while you train

Many students keep their job through barber school, and the schedules are built for that. The afternoon class runs 2 PM to 8 PM, which can leave mornings free for work. The weekend class runs Saturday and Sunday, which can leave your weekdays open for a full-time job. It takes longer to finish (about 27 weeks instead of 17), but you never have to give up your paycheck.

A simple way to plan: pick the schedule that fits your work hours, figure out your weekly payment, and check that your income covers it along with your regular bills. If it's tight, the weekend schedule's longer timeline spreads tuition out over more weeks. For more on balancing the two, see going to barber school while working full-time.

Questions to ask any barber school about cost

  • What is the total tuition for my schedule, and what does it include?
  • How much is the down payment, and what is the weekly amount?
  • What tools, books and supplies do I need, and can I buy them elsewhere?
  • Do you work with ACCES-VR? Are you approved for VA benefits at the campus I'll attend?
  • Is the school licensed by the New York State Education Department?
