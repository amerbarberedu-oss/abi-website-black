No High School Diploma? How the ATB Exam Can Get You Into Barber School

From the American Barber Institute blog.

Yes, you can go to barber school in New York without a high school diploma. At American Barber Institute, applicants need a high school diploma or GED, or they can pass the Ability-To-Benefit (ATB) entrance exam at ABI instead. Pass the ATB, meet the other admission requirements, and you can enroll in the 500-Hour Master Barber program like any other student. Here's how it works and what else you'll need.

What is the ATB exam?

ATB stands for Ability-To-Benefit. It's an entrance exam that gives applicants without a high school diploma or GED another way to qualify for admission. Instead of showing a diploma, you show through the exam that you're ready to take on the program.

At ABI, the ATB exam is taken at the school as part of the admissions process. For the details, such as when you can take it and what to bring, call admissions or stop by. The office is open Monday to Friday, 8 AM to 8 PM, and walk-ins are welcome.

Who is the ATB exam for?

The ATB exam is for anyone who wants to become a barber but doesn't have a high school diploma or GED. That covers a lot of people, for a lot of reasons:

  • You left school early to work or help support your family.
  • Life got in the way, and you never went back for a GED.
  • You've been cutting hair for friends and family for years and you're ready to make it official.
  • You're starting over and want a trade you can build a career on.

None of these should keep you out of the barbershop. Barbering rewards skill, consistency and how you treat people. The ATB route exists so that missing a diploma doesn't block you from even starting.

What else do you need to enroll in barber school?

The diploma (or ATB) is only one piece. To enroll at ABI, you'll also need:

  • Age: at least 17. If you're under 18, you need parental permission.
  • Social Security card or Tax ID.
  • Proof of address.
  • Photo ID.

If you do have a diploma or GED, bring it, and you won't need the ATB exam. Our How to Get Started page walks through enrollment step by step, and the FAQ answers the most common admission questions.

What if my diploma is from another country?

A foreign high school diploma can count. You'll need to bring a notarized translation of the diploma along with a copy of the original. If you're not sure your documents will be accepted, ask ABI's admissions team before you apply.

If English isn't your first language, ABI's Bronx campus offers bilingual instruction, and there's a dedicated Spanish line, (212) 290-0278, for questions in Spanish.

Does not having a diploma affect getting your barber license?

The diploma question comes up when you apply to school. For the license itself, the New York Department of State lists these requirements: be at least 17, pass a physical exam signed by a physician, physician's assistant or nurse practitioner, complete a barber course at a NYS-approved school, complete the one-time state-approved contagious diseases course, and pass the state practical exam. If you have questions about your specific situation, check with the Division of Licensing Services.

For the full process, read How to Get a Barber License in New York: Step-by-Step Guide.

What happens after you're admitted?

Once you're in, you're a barber student like everyone else. The 500-hour program starts with about two weeks of theory: sanitation and sterilization, barber history, NY barber law and shop management. Then it's hands-on: tapers, fades, caesars, razor lineups, beard trims, hot-towel shaves and more. Within the first few weeks, you start working on real members of the public in ABI's supervised student clinic. State Board exam prep is built into the program.

You can pick the schedule that fits your life:

  • Morning: Monday to Friday, 8 AM to 2 PM, about 17 weeks.
  • Afternoon: Monday to Friday, 2 PM to 8 PM, about 17 weeks (the most popular).
  • Weekend: Saturday and Sunday, 9 AM to 7 PM, about 27 weeks.

Tuition and payment options are covered in how to pay for barber school in NYC.

Tuition can be paid through weekly payment plans from $160 a week, from $200 down. Tools, books and supplies are extra. New classes start the first Monday of every month.

Paying for barber school without a diploma

Weekly payment plans are open to every student. If you have a documented disability, New York's ACCES-VR program may cover tuition, fees, books and tools for eligible New Yorkers. If you're a veteran, ABI's Manhattan campus is approved for several VA education benefits, including the Post-9/11 GI Bill; you'll need a Certificate of Eligibility and your DD-214. VA benefits are available at the Manhattan campus only.

Your next step

A missing diploma is a paperwork problem, not a talent problem. If you want to cut hair for a living, the ATB exam is a practical way in. ABI has trained 10,000+ graduates since 1996, and its job placement office meets one-on-one with every graduate to connect them with shops that are hiring.
