Going to Barber School While Working Full-Time: Evening and Weekend Options

From the American Barber Institute blog.

Yes, you can go to barber school while working full-time. The key is choosing a schedule that doesn't overlap your job. In New York City, that usually means either an afternoon-into-evening class on weekdays or a weekend-only class. At American Barber Institute, the afternoon schedule runs Monday to Friday, 2 PM to 8 PM, and the weekend schedule runs Saturday and Sunday, 9 AM to 7 PM. Both lead to the same 500-hour program and the same State Board preparation. The trade-off is time: the weekend route keeps your weekdays free but takes longer to finish.

Can you do barber school online or part-time?

Not online. New York requires barber training to be done in person, at a state-approved school, because the skills are hands-on and you have to practice on real people under supervision. There is no legitimate online barber school for a New York license.

"Part-time," though, is very possible, as long as you are realistic about what it means. You still have to complete all 500 hours. A schedule with fewer hours per week simply spreads those hours over more weeks.

Option 1: The afternoon schedule (Mon–Fri, 2 PM–8 PM)

The afternoon class is 30 hours a week and takes about 17 weeks, or roughly four months. At $3,600 it is also the lowest-priced schedule and the most popular one at ABI.

Who it works for: people whose jobs end by early afternoon. Think early shifts in delivery, warehouses, building maintenance, food prep, security or anything that starts very early in the morning. It can also work for people who work nights and want their afternoons and early evenings for school, though you'll need to be honest with yourself about sleep.

Who it doesn't work for: anyone with a standard 9-to-5 office job. The hours overlap, and showing up late every day means missing training.

Option 2: The weekend schedule (Sat–Sun, 9 AM–7 PM)

The weekend class is 18 hours a week and takes about 27 weeks, or six to seven months. Tuition is $4,600.

Who it works for: people with a Monday-to-Friday job who want to keep it untouched. You don't have to ask for schedule changes or cut your hours. You come in on Saturday and Sunday and keep your weekday routine.

What it costs you: your weekends, for about half a year. Two ten-hour days back to back is a real commitment, and it means less time for family, rest and errands. It's worth talking this through with the people you live with before you start.

What about the morning schedule?

ABI also offers a morning class, Monday to Friday from 8 AM to 2 PM ($5,600, about 17 weeks). It's a good fit for people who work evenings or nights, such as restaurant, bar or overnight workers, and for anyone who can study full-time. For most 9-to-5 workers, though, the choice comes down to afternoon or weekend. You can compare all three on the schedules page.

The trade-off: time to finish

All three schedules cover the same 500 hours. The difference is how fast you get through them:

  • Morning or afternoon: 30 hours a week, about 17 weeks.
  • Weekend: 18 hours a week, about 27 weeks.

Finishing in four months instead of six or seven means you can take the State Board practical exam and start earning as a licensed barber sooner. On the other hand, the weekend schedule lets you keep your full income the whole time, which for many people is the only way school is possible. Neither is "better." The right choice is the one you can actually keep up with every week until you're done. For a full breakdown of timelines, see how long barber school takes in New York.

How to plan a realistic week

Before you enroll, write out a normal week hour by hour: work, commute, school, sleep, meals, family and anything else you can't skip. A few tips that help:

  • Count the commute. ABI's Manhattan campus is at 48 West 39th Street near Bryant Park; the Bronx campus is at 121 Westchester Square, near the 6 train and the Bx31/Bx40 buses. Choose the campus that makes the trip from work or home easiest.
  • Protect your sleep. Clippers and razors require focus. Being exhausted makes it harder to learn and less safe to practice.
  • Plan your meals. A 2 PM–8 PM class right after a work shift is a long day. Pack food.
  • Talk to your employer if it helps. You don't have to, but some people find that a small shift change makes everything easier.
  • Practice outside class when you can. Even short sessions on friends and family help your hands remember what you learned.

Paying for school while you work

One of the biggest advantages of keeping your job is that you can pay tuition out of your regular income. ABI offers weekly payment plans from $160 a week, starting from $200 down, so the cost lines up with your paycheck. Remember that tools, books and supplies are extra. You'll get the tool list at registration and can buy from ABI or elsewhere. Our guide to paying for barber school in NYC covers payment plans, ACCES-VR and VA benefits in more detail.

Is it worth the effort?

For a few months, your schedule will be full. That is the honest truth. But barber school is a defined, short commitment with a clear finish line: 500 hours, then the State Board exam, then a New York barber license that lets you work behind the chair. Many people use the stability of their current job to get through training and then move into barbering once they're licensed. After graduation, ABI's job placement office meets one-on-one with every graduate to help connect them with shops that are hiring.

New classes start the first Monday of every month (the Tuesday when a federal holiday falls on that Monday), so you never have to wait long.
