How to Get a Barber License in New York: Step-by-Step Guide

From the American Barber Institute blog.

To get a barber license in New York, you need to be at least 17, pass a physical exam, complete a barber course at a New York State-approved school (or a two-year registered apprenticeship), take the one-time contagious diseases course, apply to the NY Department of State, and pass the state practical exam. The initial license application fee is $40 and the exam fee is $15. Below is every step, in order, with the details people usually trip over.

What license do you actually need?

New York's Department of State (DOS), through its Division of Licensing Services, issues the license. Officially it's a "barber" license, sometimes called an operator license, and it's the license that lets you cut hair for pay. Schools and the industry commonly call it the Master Barber license, so if you see both terms, they mean the same thing.

That license is different from a Barber Shop license, which is what you need to own or operate a shop. There's also an area renter license for independent barbers who rent space inside someone else's shop. Most new barbers start with the operator license only, then add a shop or area renter license later if their career goes that way.

Step-by-step: how to become a licensed barber in New York

  1. Meet the age requirement. You must be at least 17. (At ABI, students under 18 need parental permission to enroll.)
  2. Choose your route to eligibility. Most people complete a barber course at a NYS-approved school. The alternative is two years (24 months) as a registered barber apprentice working under a licensed supervising barber. There are also routes for out-of-state education or experience, and reciprocity with Maine, New Mexico and Pennsylvania.
  3. Complete your training. Hours are set by the approved schools. At American Barber Institute, the Master Barber program is 500 hours, which most students finish in about 17 weeks on a weekday schedule.
  4. Take the contagious diseases course. Every new barber and barber apprentice applicant must complete the state-approved infection control course. It's one time only, not something you repeat at renewal.
  5. Get your physical. A physician, physician's assistant or nurse practitioner signs the health certification on your application. Watch the clock here: you must submit the application within 30 days of that signature.
  6. Apply to the Department of State. Submit your application with the $40 initial license fee.
  7. Schedule and pass the practical exam. After your application is approved, DOS emails you a link to schedule the practical exam online. The $15 exam fee is paid when you schedule. You need to pass the practical exam within two years.
  8. Receive your license and start working. Once you pass, you're licensed to work as a barber in New York.

School or apprenticeship: which route is faster?

For most people, school is faster. An apprenticeship requires a full two years under a supervising barber before you're eligible. A 500-hour program like ABI's takes about 17 weeks on a weekday schedule (morning or afternoon, 30 hours a week) or about 27 weeks on weekends. You also train with structured theory, a supervised clinic and exam preparation, instead of learning only what comes through one shop's door. For a full breakdown, see How Long Does Barber School Take in New York?

The apprenticeship route can make sense if you already have a licensed barber willing to supervise you and you're committed to the full two years. Either way, the contagious diseases course is still required.

The contagious diseases course

This is the step people forget until the last minute. New York requires every new barber (operator) or barber apprentice applicant to complete a state-approved infection control and contagious diseases course. ABI offers a 3-hour Contagious Diseases course as home study by mail for $100. It's a one-time requirement, so once it's done, it's done.

How the practical exam works

You can't schedule the exam until DOS approves your application. Once it does, you'll get an email with a scheduling link and pay the $15 non-refundable fee online. A few rules matter:

  • You can reschedule for free if you do it at least 6 days before your exam.
  • Cancelling, not showing up, or arriving late forfeits your fee, and late arrivals are not admitted.
  • Bring a current, unexpired government-issued photo ID with your signature. The name has to match your appointment.
  • If you need testing accommodations, you can request them using form DOS-1591.

DOS publishes the practical exam supply list, the list of tasks evaluated and the draping procedures on dos.ny.gov. Review all three well before your date. We go deeper in The NY Barber Practical Exam: What to Expect and How to Prepare.

How much does a New York barber license cost?

The state fees themselves are modest:

  • Initial license application: $40
  • Practical exam: $15 (non-refundable)
  • Renewal: $40
  • Late renewal penalty: $10

The bigger cost is training. At ABI, the 500-hour program runs $3,600 for the afternoon schedule, $4,600 for weekends and $5,600 for mornings, with weekly payment plans from $160 a week and from $200 down. Tools, books and supplies are extra. Add the cost of your physical and the $100 contagious diseases course if you take it through ABI. ABI's financial aid options include payment plans, ACCES-VR and VA benefits (VA is Manhattan campus only).

Renewing your barber license

Renewal is done online and costs $40. If you renew late, there's a $10 penalty on top of that. For exact renewal timing and any current requirements, check with the Division of Licensing Services, since DOS sets those rules.

Operator license vs. shop license

Your barber license covers you as a person. It doesn't cover a business. If you open your own shop, the shop needs its own Barber Shop license. If you work independently by renting space, look into the area renter license. Many barbers work under their operator license for a few years, build a clientele, then move into renting or ownership. That's part of why earnings tend to grow over time, though they always vary and are never guaranteed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a barber license cost in New York?

The state fees are $40 for the initial license application and $15 for the practical exam, which is non-refundable. Renewal costs $40, plus a $10 penalty if you renew late. Training is the bigger cost: at American Barber Institute the 500-hour program starts at $3,600, with weekly payment plans available.

Can I get a barber license in New York without going to barber school?

Yes. Instead of a school course, you can complete a two-year (24-month) registered barber apprenticeship under a licensed supervising barber. There are also routes for out-of-state education or experience, and reciprocity with Maine, New Mexico and Pennsylvania.

How old do you have to be to get a barber license in New York?

You must be at least 17 to get a New York barber license. At American Barber Institute, students under 18 also need parental permission to enroll.

Do I have to retake the contagious diseases course when I renew my barber license?

No. The state-approved contagious diseases (infection control) course is a one-time requirement for new barber and barber apprentice applicants. ABI offers it as a 3-hour home-study course by mail for $100.

How long do I have to pass the NY barber practical exam?

You must pass the practical exam within two years. You can only schedule it after the NY Department of State approves your license application and emails you a scheduling link.

Do I need a separate license to open my own barbershop?

Yes. Your barber license covers you as an individual, while a shop needs its own Barber Shop license. Barbers who rent space inside someone else's shop should look into the area renter license.
