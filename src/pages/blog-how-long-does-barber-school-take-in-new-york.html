How Long Does Barber School Take in New York?

From the American Barber Institute blog.

Barber school in New York usually takes about four to seven months, depending on your schedule. At American Barber Institute, the Master Barber program is 500 hours: about 17 weeks (roughly four months) if you train weekdays, or about 27 weeks (six to seven months) if you train on weekends. After that, you apply to the state and pass the practical exam to get licensed. Here's how the time breaks down and what fills those hours.

How many hours is barber school in New York?

New York's Department of State requires you to complete a barber course at a NYS-approved school, and the hours are set by the approved schools. ABI's Master Barber program is 500 hours, offered at both the Manhattan and Bronx campuses. Since total hours are fixed, the real question is how many hours a week you can put in. That's what determines whether you finish in four months or closer to seven.

17 weeks or 27 weeks: it depends on your schedule

ABI runs three schedules. All three cover the same 500-hour program; the difference is pace.

  • Morning: Monday to Friday, 8 AM to 2 PM. 30 hours a week, about 17 weeks. $5,600.
  • Afternoon: Monday to Friday, 2 PM to 8 PM. 30 hours a week, about 17 weeks. $3,600. This is the most popular schedule.
  • Weekend: Saturday and Sunday, 9 AM to 7 PM. 18 hours a week, about 27 weeks. $4,600.

If speed matters most, a weekday schedule gets you to the finish line fastest. If you're working full time Monday to Friday, the weekend schedule takes longer but lets you keep your paycheck while you train. New classes start the first Monday of every month (or the Tuesday, when a federal holiday falls on that Monday), so you never have to wait long to begin.

What fills the 500 hours?

Barber school is mostly hands-on, but it starts with the foundation.

The first two weeks: theory

Roughly the first two weeks cover the knowledge every licensed barber needs: sanitation and sterilization, barber history, New York barber law and shop management. It's the groundwork for working safely and legally on the public.

The rest: hands-on cutting, shaving and styling

Then you get behind the chair. Students learn classic tapers; low, mid and high fades; high-top fades; caesars; bald heads; fohawks; pompadours; afros; flat tops; mohawks; razor lineups and shape-ups; beard trims; scissor and clipper cuts; shampoos; blowouts; hot-towel shaves and facial massage.

Real clients, early

Students start working on real members of the public in ABI's supervised student clinic within the first few weeks. That's where a lot of the 500 hours really go: repetition on different hair types, head shapes and requests, with instructors watching and correcting. By graduation, a fade isn't something you've studied. It's something you've done many times.

State Board exam prep

Preparation for the New York State practical exam is built into the program, so you're ready for the next step when you finish.

One thing to know: training is in person only. New York requires it, so there is no online barber school for a NY license.

How long from graduation to license?

Finishing school doesn't make you licensed on its own. A few more steps come after (or alongside) your training:

  1. Complete the one-time, state-approved contagious diseases course. ABI offers it as a 3-hour home-study course by mail.
  2. Get your physical signed by a physician, physician's assistant or nurse practitioner, and submit your application within 30 days of that signature, with the $40 fee.
  3. Wait for the Department of State to approve your application. DOS then emails you a link to schedule the practical exam.
  4. Schedule the exam online ($15) and pass it.

How long the state takes to process your application and how soon exam dates are available isn't something a school controls, so check with the Division of Licensing Services for current timing. You can save time on your end by finishing the contagious diseases course early and having your paperwork ready the moment you graduate. School vs. apprenticeship: how long does each take?

New York also lets you qualify through a registered barber apprenticeship: two years (24 months) working under a licensed supervising barber. That's two full years before you're eligible, compared with about four months on a weekday school schedule or six to seven months on weekends.

An apprenticeship can work for someone who already has a supervising barber lined up. But for most people who want to get licensed and start earning, school is the shorter path, and it comes with structured theory, a clinic full of real clients and exam preparation.

Is it worth the time?

Four to seven months is a short runway for a skilled trade. Barbers' earnings vary a lot, but the estimates on ABI's site run from about $35,000 to $45,000 in year one to $50,000 to $70,000 in years two and three, and about $75,000 to $100,000+ from year three on for booth renters and shop owners. None of that is guaranteed; it depends on your skill, your clientele and how hard you work.
